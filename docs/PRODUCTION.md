Run 1 is the selected setup: `gemini-3.8-flash` without reasoning, Legal detail style, 1,000 end-to-end cases (`evals/results/e2e_all_gemini-3.8-flash_legal.json`, 2026-11-20).

**Weak spots measured, not hidden:**
- **"The Act does not cover this":** of 30 questions on topics the Act does not regulate, 15 were answered correctly that the Act does not cover them and 1 was answered from loosely related provisions. The other 14 were refused by the guard as out of scope: harmless, but a less helpful reply than "the Act does not regulate this", and counted as failures.
- **Ambiguous questions:** 22 of 40 got a clarifying question. The human review rated the conditional answers to the rest as good (8–10). The label may be too strict, but the behaviour should be decided with users.
- **Judge vs human:** they agree weakly (Pearson 0.09 per answer), so the LLM judge can catch failures but cannot choose between near-equal setups. Human review remains necessary.

Cost per traffic level, model and hosting, is in §3.

## 2. Risks and mitigations

- **The law changes and answers use outdated text.** The 2026 amendments exist; the prototype uses the 12.7.2024 text.
  *Mitigation:* re-ingest each new consolidated version and re-run the evaluations before publishing it; the UI already flags the text date.
- **A wrong legal answer causes harm.**
  *Mitigation:* answers only from retrieved excerpts; mandatory citations, checked against the excerpts; "not legal advice" for personal situations; block or regenerate answers whose citations fail; a legal expert reviews the evaluation set.
- **Questions needing several provisions (Art. 6 → Annex III → Arts. 8–15) are answered partially.**
  *Mitigation:* cross-references are already extracted per chunk (`refs`); add one-hop expansion along them and 20–30 hand-written multi-provision test cases.
- **The evaluation is optimistic.** Questions were generated by an LLM from single chunks, by the same model family as the answer model.
  *Mitigation:* collect real (anonymised) questions; a legal expert writes a gold set; keep a judge outside that family (already the case).
- **Dependence on one provider** (Gemini for embeddings, guard and answers).
  *Mitigation:* Mistral Large 4 is integrated and evaluated (switch by setting `ANSWER_MODEL` and `MISTRAL_API_KEY`; not automatic); search falls back to BM25 + resolver if embeddings fail; automatic failover to be added.
- **Personal data in questions sent to a third-party API (GDPR).**
  *Mitigation:* data processing agreement, EU data residency where offered, retention limit on stored conversations; today conversations are stored with a random session ID only, no accounts or IP addresses.
- **Prompt injection beyond what regex and the system prompt catch.**
  *Mitigation:* 0 of 120 obeyed today; add an injection classifier and output filters; the model has no tools or data to leak beyond the public Act.
- **EUR-Lex blocks scripted downloads.**
  *Mitigation:* use the official CELLAR / EUR-Lex web services, or a documented manual download, with hashes as today.
- **Cost or abuse spikes on a public URL.**
  *Mitigation:* rate limits per client, a global daily cap and provider spend limits (in place); raise the global cap only step by step with traffic (§3.2).

## 3. Scalability

### 3.1 Running cost by traffic

**Model cost.** Assumptions: 80% of questions in scope and answered, 20% stopped early (almost free when the relevance floor stops them); the token use of run 1 (1,780 input / 250 output tokens per answer, 420 / 70 for the guard); the answer-model price doubling on 1 Jan 2027 (`app/pricing.yaml`, confirmed on the Gemini pricing page on 2026-11-22). Real conversations carry history, so a **+30% margin** is added.

**Hosting cost on Railway.** Prices from railway.com/pricing (read 2026-11-22): memory $10 per GB-month, CPU $20 per vCPU-month, volumes $0.15 per GB-month, egress $0.05 per GB, billed per second. The Hobby plan costs $5 a month and includes $5 of usage; Pro costs $20 and includes $20. Measured: the app uses about 105 MB of memory with the index loaded; an answer sends about 1 KB of text and the page is 65 KB, so egress stays under $1 a month even at the largest tier. CPU per request was not measured: the app mostly waits on the model API, so CPU use is **estimated** as low.

| Traffic | Questions / month | Model, today | Model, from Jan 2027 | Railway set-up | Railway / month (**estimate**) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot, 500 / day | 15,000 | ~$40 | ~$75 | Hobby: 1 container, SQLite on a 1 GB volume | ~$5 (usage stays within the included $5) |
| Production, 10,000 / day | 300,000 | ~$800 | ~$1,500 | Pro: 2 app replicas, Postgres, Redis | ~$25–35 |
| Large, 100,000 / day | 3,000,000 | ~$8,000 | ~$15,000 | Pro: 4–6 app replicas, larger Postgres | ~$80–150 |

Hosting is a small share at every tier: about $5 against $40 of model cost for the pilot, and under 5% of the model cost from the production tier up. Not included: external monitoring or tracing tools, and sampled judge scoring of live answers (2% of answers at about $0.0026 each, about $15 a month at the production tier).

### 3.2 What has to change as traffic grows

- **App container:** one container today; add replicas as traffic grows (Railway Pro allows up to 42 per service). A load test sets how many each tier needs.
- **Database:** conversations are in a SQLite file, which works with one container only; move to Postgres, shared by all replicas.
- **Traffic limits per user:** the per-client limits and the global cap of 1,500 questions a day are counted in each container's memory; move the counts to Redis so all replicas share them, and raise the global cap step by step with traffic, keeping it as the safety net against runaway cost.
- **Stored conversations:** they grow with traffic; set a retention period (for example 30 days), which GDPR needs anyway.

### 3.3 Further development ideas

Not part of the cost figures above.

- **Answer cache for repeated questions.** Questions like "what are the fines?" repeat often in a legal tool. Serving a stored answer for the same (rewritten) question costs no model call and answers instantly. It must be cleared whenever the corpus or the prompt changes, and the hit rate is unknown until real traffic exists.
- **User accounts.** Login would give each user their own conversation history across devices and sessions, and per-user quotas instead of per-IP limits. Today conversations are anonymous and tied to a browser session.
- **Multilingual answers.** The design keeps one English index and swaps in the official text of the same provisions in the user's language (METHODOLOGY §8.4); the retrieval half was tested in E8 for Lithuanian, Polish, Finnish and French. Building it and validating answers with native speakers is future work.
